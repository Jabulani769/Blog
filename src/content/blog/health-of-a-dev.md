Catching the green dot is important to most developers, we most of the times forget about ourselves. We spend counteless hours (countless, really?) chasing useful and useless solutions, fixing bugs, keeping the mighty streak alive. <i>"Just one more commit"</i>, while this can make you feel dedicated but it costs us something far more important: OUR HEALTH.

## Physical fitness and Mental health

As it sounds these are two different terms, one is about the condition of your body (physical) and the other is avout your mind. Developer wise these two influence each other. A neglected body leads to a tired body, unfocused mind which ends up you writing some sloppy code.Being out of shape doesn't only mean being overweight. For developers or any mouse potatos out there, sitting for hours slows blood circulation, poor posture strains the neck n back and also lack of movement drains energy, slowly your mind becomes foggy. best scenario case: <mark class="bg-amber-100"> You reread the same line of code over n over n bugs that should take you minutes take hours to fix then irritation creeps in.

</mark> Come to think of it, most of these habits are not done intentionally. They are a side effect deep focus and a little pressure. We replace meals with caffeine. We push late into the night, convincing ourselves sleep can wait. the problem is we forget that the body keeping us productive is not some alien tech (not infinite).

## Why fitness and mental health matters

Taking care of your body is not a distraction from coding/working. Think of it as part of the job. When you move regularly, sleep better and manage stress, you think clearer. You debug faster, you get less frustrated. You dont need to code the longest hours to be categorized as the best developer. Some of the simple exercises that can really make a difference in out work flow:

<ol>
    <li>Neck stretches, do it in between tasks it releases tension.</li>
    <li>Sholder rolls, I recommende doing it hourly it improves posture.</li>
    <li>Desk push-ups, doing it when breaking to regain that upper body strength. </li>
    <li>Short walks, after long sessions of work. Its the best method when it comes to mental reset.</li>
</ol>

Old age feels abstract, but habits compound. The body you build now (in the twenties and thirties, though is never too late) determines how you move, think and live decades later. One day the commits will slow down, the job titles will change. What remains is the health. Just don't destroy yourself in the process.
